Broiler margin over grain

Benchmark poultry price minus the corn and soybean-meal cost of producing a pound of ready-to-cook carcass weight, monthly since 2003, in constant dollars. Every number comes from a public series published through FRED — nothing proprietary, nothing behind a login, all of it checkable at the links at the bottom.

This tracks a global benchmark spread, not any company's realised margin. A processor's actual realisation depends on its cut mix, further processing, contracts and channel, and its actual feed cost depends on local basis — none of which is public. What moves here is the commodity backdrop every US processor sits inside, not anyone's P&L.

What moved the margin

A margin is two things moving at once, and they rarely move together. Splitting the last twelve months into the part that came from the poultry price and the part that came from feed cost says which side is actually under pressure.

Method, and what this does not claim

Grain cost per pound of ready-to-cook carcass weight = (corn share × corn price + soy-meal share × soy-meal price) ÷ 2,204.62 lb per tonne × (feed conversion ÷ dressing yield). Spread = benchmark poultry price − that number. Both sides are then deflated by CPI to 2026-06 dollars.

  • It is a benchmark spread, not a company margin. It says nothing about any processor's realised price, which depends on cut mix, further processing, contract and channel terms and freight — none of it public.
  • "Ready-to-cook carcass weight", not "sellable product". Feed conversion is a live-weight ratio; dividing by dressing yield converts it to carcass weight. Getting to a saleable cut basis would need a further product-yield assumption that is not made here.
  • Everything is in constant dollars, and that materially changes the answer. The nominal series ranks near its 23-year top; deflated it is meaningfully lower, and against the last ten years it is close to the middle. A percentile computed on nominal prices across 23 years mostly measures inflation.
  • This is an indicator of direction and compression, not a cost accounting figure. It prices two ingredients, not a P&L.
  • Excluded from the cost side: roughly a tenth of ration weight — fats, minerals, vitamins, synthetic amino acids — plus processing, labour, energy, freight, chick cost and grower pay.
  • IMF benchmarks are global, not delivered-to-Fort-Smith. The basis between a global benchmark and a US delivered cash price is real, so the level here sits below a US processor's true cost. Movement is what this tracks.
  • Corn and soybean meal drive nearly all the variance in ration cost, which is why the series tracks margin movement well despite pricing only two inputs.
  • The correlations use overlapping twelve-month windows, so ordinary significance tests do not apply to them. A three-month-change version, which overlaps far less, is computed as a check and points the same way at a shorter lead.
  • Monthly, and the benchmark publishes with a lag. The most recent month here is 2026-06. This is not a daily trading signal.
  • Two further series were dropped on purpose. Two BLS producer-price series returned usable data, but their exact definitions could not be confirmed from here, and an unverified label on a public page is worse than a missing one.
